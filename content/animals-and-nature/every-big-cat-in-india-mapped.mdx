India is home to four big cats in the wild: the tiger, the Asiatic lion, the leopard and the snow leopard. Each is counted by a different survey, on a different cycle. This story brings the latest official numbers together.

## Tigers: more than doubled since 2006

The All India Tiger Estimation is run every four years by the National Tiger Conservation Authority and the Wildlife Institute of India. It uses camera traps, sign surveys and statistical models.

The gains are not even. Madhya Pradesh added 259 tigers between 2018 and 2022. Arunachal Pradesh fell from 29 to 9. Odisha, Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand each recorded fewer tigers than in 2018.

## Asiatic lions: one landscape, spreading out

The Asiatic lion survives in the wild only in Gujarat. The state counts its lions every five years.

The 2025 count found 891 lions, up 32% from 2020. Only 394 were in the core Gir and Pania area. The rest live in satellite populations across Saurashtra, including the coast and Bhavnagar district. Many of these lions now live on farmland and wasteland near villages.

This is good news for numbers and a risk for the species. A single disease outbreak or disaster in one region could still hit the whole population.

## Leopards: the most widespread big cat

The 2022 leopard estimate covered about 70% of the leopard's range in India. It found 13,874 leopards, up 8% from 12,852 in 2018.

Central India and the Eastern Ghats hold the largest share. The Shivalik hills and Gangetic plains recorded a decline, from 1,253 to 1,109.

## Snow leopards: counted for the first time

The Snow Leopard Population Assessment in India ran from 2019 to 2023. It covered about 1.2 lakh square kilometres of high mountain habitat with 1,971 camera trap locations.

## How to read these numbers

Each figure is an estimate with a range, not a headcount. Methods have improved between cycles, so part of any increase reflects better surveys. The direction for tigers and lions is still clear and well documented.
